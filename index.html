Nisab & Hawl

The threshold, drawn the way you draw it.

Zakat is only due once your wealth passes a threshold set by the value of gold or silver. Which metal you use changes the answer significantly, because the two have drifted far apart in modern times.

Which metal sets the line

The default is the lower of the two — in practice, silver. This is the Hanafi rule for mixed holdings and the recommendation of the National Zakat Foundation and most charity bodies: a lower threshold means more people qualify to pay, which is the cautious position.

You can switch to silver only, fixing the threshold regardless of gold prices, or to gold only — a higher threshold that is defensible for someone whose zakatable wealth is gold alone.

Seeing where you stand

The threshold is shown in your own currency, alongside the weight and metal it came from, with a progress bar telling you how close you are to it — so a user below the line understands why nothing is owed rather than simply seeing a zero.

A countdown that follows the moon

Days elapsed, days remaining, and the due date in both the Gregorian and Hijri calendar — “12 Ramadan 1447”, not just a date in March. The countdown follows the true lunar anniversary rather than a flat 354 days, which would drift by roughly a day each year.

Paying your Zakat automatically restarts the year from the day of payment, as classical fiqh requires.

Lunar or solar, your choice

Keep a lunar year and pay 2.5%. Prefer to anchor to a Gregorian date for accounting convenience and the rate is automatically raised to 2.577% — a solar year is about eleven days longer, and the uplift keeps your lifetime obligation whole. Permitted by AAOIFI, the OIC Fiqh Academy and Kuwait’s Zakat House.

Where calculators go wrong

Live prices, gold purity and debt.

Three places an ordinary calculator quietly gets the number wrong.

Live gold & silver pricing

The threshold is a weight of metal, so it is only as good as the price behind it. BarakahFlow pulls live international spot prices, refreshing roughly every thirty seconds while the calculator is open, shown in your own currency.

A Live Market / Manual Mode switch lets you override with a local price. If the live feed is ever unavailable, the app says so and stamps the last known price with its time — it never presents a stale number as current.

Gold purity, handled properly

Gold is tracked separately at 24, 22, 21 and 18 karat — the purities people actually own across the Gulf, South Asia and Europe — in grams or ounces. Each karat is valued at its own quoted price where available, so only the pure gold content enters the calculation. Stones, settings and non-gold alloy are excluded.

You can also record how much of each karat is jewellery you actually wear, which is what allows the Hanafi and majority positions on worn adornment to produce genuinely different answers from the same holdings.

How debts reduce what you owe

This is where most calculators do real damage — by letting people deduct an entire mortgage balance and wipe out their Zakat altogether. No school permits that.

Liabilities payable in the next twelve months are deducted in full. For a mortgage you record the principal outstanding and the principal falling due within the year, then choose how much of it reduces your Zakat: nothing at all (the default and safest position), one month of principal, or twelve lunar months. The interest portion is never deductible, and the app says so plainly.

Purifying interest

If interest has accumulated in your bank account, you record it and the app removes it from your wealth base entirely — then states plainly that it is not your wealth, cannot be purified by paying Zakat on it, and must be given away separately.

A record that survives the years

Saving a calculation stores far more than a number: the complete method you were using, the metal prices at that moment, the exchange rates and the date they came from, and the Hijri dates on both ends of your year. A figure from three years ago can be reproduced exactly and defended.

How is BarakahFlow different from a normal budgeting app?

Zakat is the product, not a widget. A full rules engine sits at the centre of the app, with around fifteen genuinely contested questions each presented as an explicit choice that names the school or standards body it comes from. Islamic giving — Zakat Al-Mal, Zakat Al-Fitr, Sadaqah, Hajj and Umrah, Eid — has its own heading in the budget rather than being buried next to “miscellaneous”. Gold and multiple currencies are native to the ledger rather than bolted onto a single-currency one.

Does the Zakat calculator follow my school of thought?

The Hanafi, Shafi’i, Maliki and Hanbali positions are all encoded and attributed. Where the Hanafi school holds that worn gold jewellery is zakatable and the majority exempt it, both options are offered side by side with their reasoning. Where the Hanafi school judges the year only at its start and end and the majority require wealth to stay above the threshold throughout, both are there. The app never quietly picks one for you.

You can start from one of six regional presets and adjust anything afterwards, or answer each question individually.

How is nisab calculated?

Nisab is drawn from live international gold and silver spot prices, refreshed roughly every thirty seconds while the calculator is open and shown in your own currency. The default threshold is the lower of the two metals — in practice silver — and you can switch to gold only or silver only. Three weight conventions are available: AAOIFI at 85g gold and 595g silver, the classical Tola convention at 87.48g and 612.36g, and MUIS Singapore at 86g and 595g.

Prices are international spot rates, not local jeweller retail prices, and the app says so on screen.

How does the Hawl countdown work?

The countdown follows the true lunar anniversary resolved against the Islamic calendar, not a flat 354 days, and shows the due date in both the Gregorian and Hijri calendars. Paying your Zakat automatically restarts the year from the day of payment, as classical fiqh requires. If you prefer to anchor to a Gregorian year for accounting convenience, the rate is automatically raised to 2.577% so your lifetime obligation stays whole.

Can I deduct my mortgage from my Zakat?

Not in full — no school permits deducting an entire mortgage balance, and calculators that allow it do real damage. BarakahFlow separates debt: liabilities payable within the next twelve months are deducted in full, which the schools agree on. For a mortgage you record the principal outstanding and the principal falling due within the year, then choose how much reduces your Zakat — nothing at all (the default and safest position), one month of principal, or twelve lunar months. The interest portion of any repayment is never deductible.
